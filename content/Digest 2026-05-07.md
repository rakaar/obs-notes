## Paper 1: Acetylcholine demixes heterogeneous dopamine signals for learning and moving

**Authors:** (Nature Neuroscience, 2026)  
**Venue:** Nature Neuroscience  
**Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02227-x  
**Note:** [[2026/Acetylcholine demixes heterogeneous dopamine signals for learning and moving]]

**Summary:**
- **Dopamine signal heterogeneity:** Dopamine neurons in the dorsal striatum encode both reward prediction errors (RPEs) for learning and movement vigor, but these signals are multiplexed at single recording sites.
- **Acetylcholine dynamics:** Acetylcholine release dips during RPE-related dopamine signals but bursts during contralateral orienting movements, acting as a temporal filter.
- **Behavioral impact:** Dopamine RPEs influence subsequent behavior only when they follow cholinergic dips, suggesting acetylcholine gates dopamine's role in learning.

**Why it matters:** This study reveals how acetylcholine dynamically separates overlapping dopamine signals to coordinate distinct functions—learning and movement—providing new insight into striatal circuit mechanisms underlying behavior.

## Paper 3: Interpretability Illusions in the Generalization of Simplified Models

**Authors:** Dan Friedman et al.  
**Venue:** ICML 2024  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03656  
**Note:** [[2026/causality, prediction in neuro, ai]] (under Interpretability → Interpretability illusions)

**Summary:**
- **Simplified models may mislead:** Dimensionality reduction and other simplifications can accurately reflect in-distribution behavior but fail to capture out-of-distribution generalization in deep learning models.
- **Controlled experiments reveal gaps:** Tests on Transformer models trained on structured tasks (e.g., Dyck languages, code completion) show simplified proxies often diverge from the original model's systematic generalization.
- **Bidirectional generalization mismatches:** Simplified representations sometimes underperform (failing where the original succeeds) or overperform (generalizing better than the original model).

**Why it matters:** These findings highlight critical risks in relying on simplified interpretability tools, as they may create illusions of understanding while obscuring true model behavior in real-world, out-of-distribution scenarios.
